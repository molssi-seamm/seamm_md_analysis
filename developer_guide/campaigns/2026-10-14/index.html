2026-10-14 MD analysis, phase 1: the shared library#

The campaign’s design is in the SEAMM developer guide: MD analysis: H5MD output, a shared library and analysis steps. This package is its phase 1.

Phase 1#

The code that diffusivity_step and thermal_conductivity_step had each copied and let drift is gathered here, and both steps import it, with no change for users.

thermal_conductivity_step.analysis: statsmodels’ ccovf, acf_err, ccf_err, moving_average, create_correlation_functions. ccovf uses the same definition and scipy’s FFT correlation, so statsmodels is no longer needed.

Changes in behaviour#

  • Speed. The Helfand moments and MSDs are computed with FFTs. The old code looped over every time origin in Python, which took O(n m) time. For a heat flux of a million points with m = 10,000 that is about 2 minutes, against 0.4 s now (this Mac). For the Helfand moments of 512 molecules over 1,001 frames it was 3.3 s, against 0.09 s now. The results are the same to rounding: the tests check this against the original loops, and on real LAMMPS output from earlier jobs they agree to about 1e-15 relative.

  • Precision. A flux with a non-zero mean has a running integral that drifts linearly. The straight line is removed before the FFTs and its contribution added back exactly, so the rounding error does not grow with the length of the series.

  • Latent bugs fixed:

    • fit_slope with weights passed sigma[i:] with y[i:j], a shape error whenever the fit ended before the last point. No caller used weights.

    • fit_green_kubo_integral failed without sigma, with a NameError on very short series, and when the sampling was coarser than 0.4 ps (a zero-width moving average; this is what ended the 2025-11-19 argon demo job, Job_003234).

    • read_dump_trajectory failed on a dump with a single timed frame.

    • The MSD trace had units Å instead of Å^2 in its metadata.

  • Defects corrected (Paul, 2026-10-14: fix the older problems in phase 1). These change results:

    • fit_green_kubo_integral accepted any second exponential whose plateau was positive and less than twice the first. On an OU test series it kept one with a negative amplitude and a time constant of 46 ps, beyond the 15 ps of data, and gave 0.018 instead of about 0.5. The second exponential is now kept only if both amplitudes are positive, the two time constants differ by more than 10 %, and three times the slower one lies within the fitted data.

    • Its error was the sum of the errors of the two amplitudes. When the two time constants are nearly equal the amplitudes are individually undetermined and strongly anticorrelated, so the error came out as large as 10^6 for a value of 0.5. The error now includes their covariance.

    • All the fits treated the errors as weights even when they were zero, as they are for a single run (the spread over runs). Thermal Conductivity’s Helfand-moment fit then returned its starting guess, 1.00. A fit with missing, zero or non-finite errors is now unweighted.

    • helfand_slope stored the central difference over points k and k+2 at k+2 instead of k+1, which shifted the curve by one sample, and its error was the error of the moment, not divided by 2 dt. Both are corrected; the slope at the origin is zero, as the running Green–Kubo integral is.

    • The fourth column of species_helfand_moments, the total, was the moment of the summed velocity components, (vx + vy + vz)^2. Its cross terms average to zero only for an isotropic system, and add noise in any case. It is now the sum of the three moments, as for the MSD.

Defects found and corrected in the steps at the same time:

  • LAMMPS Heat Flux sub-step (lammps_step). To remove each atom’s average stress, the callback passed the average stress, negated, as a virial to fix external. compute stress/atom multiplies a virial by -nktv2p (-68,568 in real units), so the stress was changed by +68,568 times its average. For argon this made the heat flux about 10^4 times too large and the thermal conductivity about 4e7 W/m/K; it is now 0.1 W/m/K, as measured. For molecular systems the correction reached neither stress compute, and the convective term was counted twice (both heat/flux computes include it).

  • Diffusivity. The per-direction coefficients Dx, Dy and Dz used the 1/6 (MSD) or 1/3 (Helfand) of the total, so they were a third of the diffusion coefficient, and they got a third of the Yeh–Hummer correction. They are now D_alpha = slope / 2 (MSD) or slope (Helfand moments), and equal D for an isotropic system.
