seamm_md_analysis.electric module#

Electrical properties from equilibrium MD: the static dielectric constant and the ionic conductivity.

Both are computed from the trajectory – unwrapped positions, so molecules stay whole, and fixed (forcefield) charges:

  • the dielectric constant from the fluctuations of the dipole of the cell, for Ewald or PPPM electrostatics with conducting (“tin-foil”) boundary conditions: ε = 1 + (⟨M²⟩ − ⟨M⟩²) / (3 V ε0 kB T). For an electrolyte, M is the dipole of the neutral molecules only: the ions carry a current, not a bounded dipole.

  • the ionic conductivity from the collective displacement of the ions’ charges (Einstein–Helfand), σ = lim ⟨‖P(t) − P(0)‖²⟩ / (6 V kB t), P = Σ qᵢ rᵢ over the ions; and the Nernst–Einstein estimate from the ions’ self-diffusion, σ_NE = e² / (V kB T) Σ N_s z_s² D_s. Their ratio is the ionicity.

Positions are in Å, charges in e, volumes in Å³, temperatures in K and times in ps; results are in SI units (S/m for conductivities, m²/s for diffusion coefficients).

seamm_md_analysis.h5md module#

Writing SEAMM’s H5MD files.

An MD step writes one H5MD file per run, md.h5md, from its code’s native output when the run ends. H5MDWriter holds the layout, so that every MD step writes the same thing; the layout is described in the documentation (“The H5MD file”).

The file is written as <name>.tmp and renamed when it is complete, so an interrupted conversion never leaves a damaged file under the real name.

seamm_md_analysis.state module#

The state the MD runs sampled, and whether they can be treated as isotropic.

Every analysis step reports the state its results belong to – temperature, pressure, volume and density, each with its error over the runs – because a property measured at the wrong pressure or density belongs to that state, not the one intended:

  • an NPT run’s mean density is the equilibrium density at its pressure;

  • an NVT run’s pressure is what it is; where the bulk modulus K is known (from the volume fluctuations of an NPT run, such as the equilibration before), the equilibrium density at the target pressure is estimated as ρ_eq ≈ ρ (1 + (P_target − P) / K).

A run is treated as isotropic – one value per property from the average of its components – only when its cell is cubic and no fix makes it anisotropic (walls, fields, deformation, flow, added forces).

seamm_md_analysis.transport.multicomponent_correlation_functions(J, fluxes, m=None)[source]#

The Green–Kubo input for the heat flux of a mixture.

In a mixture the heat flux is correlated with the interdiffusion of the species, and the Green–Kubo integral of its autocorrelation function gives too large a thermal conductivity. The thermal conductivity comes instead from the Schur complement of the matrix of Onsager coefficients (Bertossa, Grasselli, Ercole and Baroni, Phys. Rev. Lett. 122, 255901 (2019)):

\[\kappa \propto L_{ee} - L_{eJ} L_{JJ}^{-1} L_{Je}, \qquad L_{ab}(t) = \int_0^t \langle a(\tau) b(0) \rangle \, d\tau\]

with e the heat flux and J the mass fluxes of all the species but one (the last is fixed by the total momentum, which is zero). Their scale cancels, so any units, or the momenta of the species, will do. The complement is taken for each lag and each direction, coupling the fluxes in the same direction, as for an isotropic fluid.

This is for one run. The complement of a short run is noisy, since L_JJ is small and poorly known; for several runs or blocks, average their flux_correlation_matrices() and take the complement of the average.

Parameters:

  • J (numpy.ndarray(3, n)) – The x, y and z components of the heat flux.

  • fluxes (numpy.ndarray(k, 3, n)) – The mass fluxes of k species, with k one less than the number of species.

  • m (int) – The number of lags to keep; all of them by default.

Returns:

  • numpy.ndarray(6, m) – The correlation functions, in the order of seamm_md_analysis.correlate.TENSOR_COMPONENTS. The diagonal ones are the derivatives of the corrected integrals (effective_correlation_functions()); the off-diagonal ones are those of the heat flux alone, which are zero either way for an isotropic system.

  • numpy.ndarray(6, m) – Their running integrals, in units of the sampling interval.

Time-series statistics, correlation functions, Green–Kubo and Helfand transport coefficients, plots, SEAMM’s H5MD files, and readers for older MD output.
