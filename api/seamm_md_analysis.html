seamm_md_analysis.transport.fit_green_kubo_integral(y, xs, sigma=None)[source]#

Fit a Green–Kubo running integral by one or two rising exponentials.

The plateau of the integral, a = sum of the amplitudes, is the transport coefficient. A single exponential a (1 - exp(-t / tau)) is fitted first over the initial rise; a second exponential is then added over tau / 2 to 20 tau. The second exponential is kept only if both amplitudes are positive, the two time constants differ, the slower one is short enough for the plateau to be reached within the fitted data (3 tau), and the value is no more than twice that of the single exponential. Otherwise the single exponential is used.

The standard error of the value includes the covariance of the two amplitudes, which are strongly correlated.

Parameters:

  • y (numpy.ndarray(n)) – The running integral.

  • xs (numpy.ndarray(n) or [float]) – The times.

  • sigma (numpy.ndarray(n)) – The standard error of y (optional). Without it, or if any value is zero or not finite (as for the spread of a single run), the fit is unweighted and the errors come from the residuals.

Returns:

The plateau value and its standard error, the amplitudes and time constants with their errors, and the fitted curve.

Return type:

GreenKuboFit

seamm_md_analysis.transport.windowed_displacement_products(Ga, Gb, m, n_origins)[source]#

Sum over time origins of the products of displacements.

\[S(k) = \sum_{i=0}^{N_0 - 1} (G_a[i+k] - G_a[i]) (G_b[i+k] - G_b[i])\]

for lags k = 0 .. m-1 and a fixed set of N_0 origins, computed with FFTs. With G the running integral of a flux, S/N_0 is the Helfand moment.

Each series is split into a straight line and the rest before the FFTs, and the line’s contribution is added back exactly. A flux with a non-zero mean has a running integral that drifts linearly, and without this the rounding error would grow as the square of the length of the series.

Parameters:

  • Ga, Gb (numpy.ndarray(n, …)) – The series, with time along the first axis. They may be the same array.

  • m (int) – The number of lags.

  • n_origins (int) – The number of time origins, N_0; n_origins + m - 1 must not exceed n.

Returns:

S for each lag and column.

Return type:

numpy.ndarray(m, …)
